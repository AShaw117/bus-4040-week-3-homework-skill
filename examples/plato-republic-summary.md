# Plato, *Republic* Book V (453a-457d): Can Women Be Guardians?

**Bottom line:** Socrates argues that women should share every duty of the
guardian class, including military training and war, because a difference of
sex is by itself irrelevant to fitness for a job. He concludes the proposal is
both feasible and best for the city.

## Key points

- **The objection.** Socrates and Glaucon earlier agreed that each person should
  do the one job they are naturally fitted for. Critics say women's nature
  differs from men's, so assigning both the same tasks contradicts that principle.
- **The "bald cobbler" reply.** Not every natural difference matters. Bald men and
  men with hair have different natures, yet it would be ludicrous to ban one group
  from making shoes. Only differences relevant to the occupation count. People who
  miss this are having a verbal competition, which Socrates calls disputation,
  rather than a real discussion.
- **What counts as aptitude.** Learning easily, discovering things for oneself
  after brief instruction, and having a body that helps rather than hinders the mind.
- **No job belongs to one sex.** Socrates claims no occupation in running a city
  belongs to a woman because she is a woman or to a man because he is a man.
  Natural aptitudes are evenly distributed between the sexes. Some women are
  natural doctors, musicians, warriors, or lovers of wisdom, just as some men are,
  and those with the guardian attributes should be guardians.
- **Caveat in the text.** Socrates repeatedly states that women are in general
  weaker than men at all these activities and should be given lighter tasks.
  Glaucon agrees, while noting that many individual women outperform individual men.
- **Same nature, same education.** Women guardians should receive the same musical
  and physical education as men and exercise naked alongside them. Anyone who
  laughs at this is "plucking the unripe fruit of laughter," since what is good
  for us is beautiful.

## Conclusions reached

- **Feasible:** yes. The proposal is not a dream. It is present-day society that
  is out of step and unnatural.
- **For the best:** yes. Guardians are the best citizens, and nothing benefits a
  city more than both its men and women becoming as good as possible.
- Socrates declares they have survived the "first wave" of criticism.

## Details worth knowing

The passage closes with Socrates introducing a "second wave," a further law
implied by the first: the guardian women shall be wives in common to the
guardian men, with no private households and children held in common. The
excerpt ends as this is stated. Translator footnotes point back to the
one-person-one-job principle at 369e-370c, the tale of Arion rescued by a
dolphin in Herodotus 1.24, and a fragment of Pindar behind the "unripe fruit" line.
